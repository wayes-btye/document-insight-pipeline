Win/loss interview transcript: Pinion Industries (lost to Apex)
Conducted by: Ben Markovich (Meridian)
Date: 29 Jul 2026
Duration: 25 min
Format: clean verbatim
Buyer: Roisin Murphy, VP Strategy, Pinion Industries

[00:00] BEN: Roisin, thanks for the time. You were under no obligation to do this so I appreciate you saying yes. The objective is purely for us to learn. Nothing you say goes outside our team and nothing we discuss is going to come back as a sales call.

ROISIN: Understood. Happy to be candid.

BEN: Great. So you closed your selection in early July. Can you walk me through how you reached the decision?

ROISIN: Sure. We started the process in April. We talked to four firms. Yourselves, Apex, one other boutique, and a larger firm we'd worked with previously who we ended up not seriously considering. By June we had it down to you and Apex.

BEN: What put us in that final two?

ROISIN: Both of you came across as genuinely thoughtful in the discovery conversation. The bigger firm was lazy with their preparation. The other boutique was fine but didn't differentiate.

BEN: What separated us from Apex in the end?

ROISIN: [pause] Three things, all related. Price. Apex was about a third less. Energy, Apex's team felt hungrier. And a specific moment where your senior person on the call said "we typically don't customise the engagement model that much" in response to a question I'd asked about flexibility. That landed badly. It made us feel we'd be slotting into your standard process whether or not it fit us.

BEN: That's useful, painful but useful. Was there anything we could have said in that moment that would have changed the outcome?

ROISIN: Maybe. If you'd come back the next week with a tailored proposal that addressed the flexibility question, it would have at least made us reconsider. As it was, we took your standard pitch as the firm view and Apex's customised pitch as theirs.

BEN: Did you do reference calls with both firms?

ROISIN: We did one with Apex, none with you. You offered to set them up but we'd decided by that point.

BEN: How did the Apex reference go?

ROISIN: Honestly, mixed. The reference customer was positive about the work but mentioned that delivery had been slower than promised. We weighed that against the pricing and the energy and decided it was acceptable.

BEN: So slower-than-promised delivery from Apex was on the table and you went with them anyway.

ROISIN: Yes. The pricing differential was big enough that some delivery slippage felt like an acceptable risk.

BEN: That's interesting. Is there a price differential at which we would have been competitive?

ROISIN: [pause] If you'd been within 15-20% of Apex on price, the customisation conversation might have flipped the decision back to you. At a third more, the maths didn't work for us.

BEN: One other thing. You mentioned customisation. What specifically did you want customised?

ROISIN: We wanted a more milestone-based engagement structure. Apex was willing to do that. Your standard was retainer.

BEN: That's a structural thing we can change. Worth knowing.

ROISIN: I'd hope so. If your standard model is the only thing on offer, you'll lose buyers like me reasonably often.

BEN: Last question. Any other feedback that would have been useful to hear before the decision?

ROISIN: Just that the gap between your discovery quality and your proposal quality was big. The discovery felt like a real conversation. The proposal felt like a template with our logo on it. Apex's proposal had clearly been written for us specifically. That mattered.

BEN: Roisin, this is exactly the feedback we needed. Thank you.

ROISIN: My pleasure. Good luck with the next pitch.

[end recording 25:08]

Ben's notes
- Three loss factors in roughly equal weight: price (33% gap), customisation flexibility, proposal differentiation
- Specific moment: senior person's "standard process" remark was the inflection point
- Reference calls were not the deciding factor (we offered, they declined)
- Apex's customised proposal contrasted directly with our templated one
- Pinion would have been winnable at 80-85% of Apex pricing with a milestone-based engagement structure
- Implications for proposal process and engagement model flexibility
