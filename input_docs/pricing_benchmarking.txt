Research snippet: pricing benchmarking - internal reference
Compiled by: David Kowalski
Date: 25 Oct 2026
For: partner discussion

Purpose: provide a defensible internal view of where our pricing sits relative to comparable firms, ahead of multiple Q4 renewal conversations where pricing is a contested point.

Methodology

Used four data sources:
1. Camden Research published rate-card data for 12 boutique commercial advisory firms (Q3 2026)
2. Direct intelligence from buyer conversations across our active client base (5 conversations referencing competitor pricing)
3. Public RFP responses from two recent procurements where we were a respondent
4. Internal cost-to-deliver model (overhead, target margin)

Findings

Day-rate positioning
- Senior consultant: we are 8-12% above sector median
- Mid-level consultant: we are at sector median
- Analyst: we are 5% below sector median

Engagement structure positioning
- We list-price retainer as the default; sector median is 60% retainer / 40% milestone or hybrid
- Apex prices significantly below us across all bands (covered in apex_brief.txt)
- One-pager / point-engagement pricing absent from our list pricing; sector median has this for $15-25k brackets

Cost-to-deliver
At current senior day-rate, we earn target margin (32%) at 70%+ senior utilisation. Below 60% utilisation, margin compresses materially.

Recommendations for partner discussion

1. Adjust list-price retainer to be the upper bound, with a 60/40 hybrid as the default offered structure. Reduces friction in procurement-led conversations.
2. Add a one-pager / point-engagement tier to the list pricing. Captures opportunities currently lost without conversation.
3. Hold senior day-rate position - the differentiation argument supports it.
4. Do not match Apex's pricing. Match Apex's engagement-model flexibility (milestone-based) where it's the actual barrier.

This is for internal reference. Not to be shared with clients or in negotiation.
