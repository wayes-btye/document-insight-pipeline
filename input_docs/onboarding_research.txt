Research snippet: onboarding friction across engagement portfolio
Compiled by: Ben Markovich
Date: 8 Oct 2026
For: onboarding redesign workstream (see note_048)

Quantitative snapshot

Across 18 engagements started in the past 24 months:
- 7 engagements (39%) had a documented onboarding-related slip of >7 days
- 4 engagements (22%) had buyer-volunteered complaints about onboarding experience
- 2 engagements (11%) lost time on the engagement was directly attributed to onboarding by the engagement lead in the close-out review

Cumulative time impact

Estimated total slipped delivery time across the 7 affected engagements: 14.5 weeks. That's roughly equivalent to 0.5 FTE of engagement-lead capacity over the 24-month period.

Buyer-volunteered phrases captured

A sample of how onboarding has been described by clients in their own words (across calls and emails):
- "rocky first 8 weeks"
- "we underestimated what we'd need to do on our side"
- "the analyst figured it out as she went"
- "I would have churned six months ago if the work itself wasn't valuable"
- "one of those things nobody complains about loudly enough to fix"

Pattern

The friction is not about Meridian's checklist or process. The friction is the assumption that the client has an internal owner with both technical fluency and commercial context. Roughly 40% of mid-market clients do not have that person available at engagement start.

Two decision pathways

If we believe this is a structural mid-market segment characteristic, we should design for it (paid guided onboarding option, or pre-engagement readiness assessment, or self-serve tooling).

If we believe this is a client-by-client lottery, we should treat it as engagement risk and load it into pricing rather than redesign the product.

The scale of the cumulative impact suggests the first interpretation.
