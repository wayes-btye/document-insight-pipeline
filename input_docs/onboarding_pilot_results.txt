Research snippet: onboarding redesign pilot - results
Compiled by: Priya Singh
Date: 16 Dec 2026
For: onboarding workstream leadership

Pilot scope

Three new engagements piloted the redesigned onboarding (note_048):
- Engagement 1: small mid-market client, started Nov
- Engagement 2: Verdant pilot (note_067)
- Engagement 3: Forge mid-market expansion scope (note_076)

Components piloted
- Pre-engagement readiness assessment (1 week, paid as line item)
- Named onboarding lead (separate from engagement lead)
- Written escalation path from week 1

Outcomes

Engagement 1: completed onboarding 4 days ahead of original timeline. Client (small mid-market) explicitly thanked us for the pre-engagement assessment, said it surfaced internal issues they hadn't anticipated.

Engagement 2 (Verdant): on time, on scope. Carmen Dalleva commented in week 2 that the engagement "felt different from the start." See note_067.

Engagement 3 (Forge): 2-day slip in week 1, recovered in week 2. Lewis commented that the named onboarding lead structure "made it easier to escalate the slip."

Quantified comparison

Across the three pilot engagements, average week-1 slip vs plan: 1.1 days. Across the prior 12 non-pilot engagements, average week-1 slip vs plan: 6.4 days.

Sample is small but the direction is clear.

Cost of redesigned onboarding

Pre-engagement readiness assessment: $5-8k incremental scope per engagement (paid by client).
Named onboarding lead overhead: ~30 hours per engagement, primarily front-loaded weeks 1-2.

Net financial impact

Estimated to be revenue-neutral or modestly positive. The added scope offsets the added overhead. Client-perceived quality has improved meaningfully.

Recommendation

Standardise across all new engagements starting Q1 2027. Stop running this as a pilot.

Open question

The pre-engagement readiness assessment surfaces issues that occasionally suggest the engagement should not proceed at the original scope. We need a clear policy for how to handle "the readiness assessment shows you're not ready" outcomes without losing the deal entirely. Suggested approach: structured optionality (smaller scope, deferred timeline, or readiness-prep work as paid sub-engagement). Worth dedicated discussion.
