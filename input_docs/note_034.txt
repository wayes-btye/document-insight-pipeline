Meeting summary: internal Apex teardown
Date: 24 Sep 2026
Attendees: Tom Whitaker (chair), Ben Markovich, Priya Singh, Marcus Chen, Sarah Patel
Compiled by: Marcus Chen

90-minute working session. Purpose: build a shared internal view of Apex Strategy Group, where they win, where they lose, and what our counter-position should be.

What we know

Drawing on the brief Ben circulated (apex_brief.txt) plus client conversations from the past quarter.

Where Apex wins
- Price-led conversations, particularly with procurement-heavy buyers
- Mid-market accounts where the buyer has limited bandwidth to evaluate depth
- Situations where the buyer has had a prior negative experience with a more expensive firm

Where Apex appears not to win
- Senior-stakeholder-led decisions (their bench is junior-heavy)
- Long-term strategic engagements requiring continuity
- Vertically-specialised work (they pitch the same offering everywhere)

What we don't know
- Their actual delivery quality. Buyers we've lost to them haven't given us post-mortem feedback.
- Their renewal rate. Sustained competitive pressure depends on whether their initial wins retain.
- Whether their data asset claim is real or marketing.

Working hypothesis

Apex is winning new business at our expense in mid-market through aggressive pricing and a credible-enough sales motion. Their win-rate likely suffers when buyers do deep evaluation. Their retention likely suffers when delivery doesn't match the sales promise.

Our counter-position should not be price-matching. It should be:
1. Force depth-of-evaluation early in the buyer's process
2. Make our senior involvement visible and quantifiable
3. Provide reference customers who explicitly chose us over Apex (we currently have one strong reference and need more)
4. Frame the buyer's decision around outcome certainty rather than input cost

What we don't have ready and need
- A simple comparison framework that helps a buyer self-assess whether they need us or Apex
- A "bake-off" engagement structure for buyers who want to try both
- Tighter feedback loop with lost-deal buyers. Currently we hear about losses through clients (note_001 etc) rather than systematically

- Tom: bring counter-position to October partner meeting for sign-off, 16 Oct
